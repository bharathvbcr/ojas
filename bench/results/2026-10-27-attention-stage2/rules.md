How it is measured (set here, before timing):

- Harness: `ojas-metal/examples/attention_stage2_bench.rs` (5 warm-ups, 25 rounds). Every arm of a row runs twice per round, once in each of two passes (A and A'); odd rounds walk the arms in reverse. Each call is timed with a sync around it. The 1-minute load average is printed at the start and the end; a run is load-clean when both are at most 6.0, and a pair is `ok` when |median(A) - median(A')| is at most the larger p90 - p10 spread of the pair (the memory-bound bench's rules). A run is clean when it is load-clean, has no NOISY pair and the control repeats; a run that is not clean is repeated (up to six attempts) and counts for nothing; every attempt of the final kernel stays in the committed output.
- Control: `linear_forward` f32 at `[5184,1024] x [4736,1024]` (fc1) is an arm of its own row in the same run. Its median TFLOP/s (2 m k n / ms) must be within 6.3 +- 3% (6.11 to 6.49), else the run is contended. Its gap to the GEMM study's 6.29 (`bench/results/2026-10-27-linear-gemm`) is reported.
- Arms: `stage 2` is `attention_forward_saving` (the dispatcher's kernel); `split-K` is `attention_forward_saving_splitk`; `pad-q` is `attention_forward_padq`, which is `attention_forward` with the pad ratio bound lifted so tessl's `encoder_attn` runs at every m (at m <= 10624 it is the route `attention_forward` takes). The saving form also computes the log-sum-exp; the pad-q arm has none of that work (a small handicap to stage 2 and split-K, as in the tiled-vs-padq bench). Pad-q has no arm where a bias or key lengths are present or the head dim is 16.
- "Against the better of split-K and pad-q" at m = 15584: the lower of the two medians in the same clean run. "Per call" is the median of the arm's A pass; the A' median is reported beside it.
- "Regress beyond noise against the kernel it replaces": at every reported row, stage 2's median minus split-K's is at most the larger of the two arms' p90 - p10. A row where it is larger is a regression and is reported as one.
- Correctness before timing, per row: stage 2 against split-K and (where it exists) pad-q over the whole output (max abs difference at most 1e-5), and against `CpuBackend` under `Numerics::Exact` on the first head plane (`max|a-b| / max|ref| <= 1e-4` and `|a-b| <= 4e-5 sqrt(Tk) + 1e-4 |ref|` per element). Dispatch counts of stage 2 and split-K on fresh uploads must be equal. A failure stops the run with no timings.
- Crossover: for m in {5200, 10400, 10624}, margin = pad-q median - stage 2 median; stage 2 beats pad-q beyond noise at m when margin > the larger p90 - p10 of the two arms and stage 2 is faster in at least 45 of the 50 paired (pass, round) samples. If it does at 10624 or below, the report gives the new crossover as the bracket those rows and m = 15584 define and says which side it falls on; `ENCODER_ATTN_MAX_PAD_RATIO` is not changed by this work.
- Transpose re-timing (the coordinator's request): in this branch's first bench job, the three transposing rows of `bench/results/2026-10-27-permute` are re-timed with `memory_bound_bench` (generic kernel and streaming reference as arms, load recorded), since the transpose grid changed to one dimension in a58410f; the results are appended to that directory's `bench-output.txt` with a note.

What would refute the result: any parity difference beyond the stated tolerances; a dispatch count unequal to split-K's; the fc1 control outside 6.11 to 6.49 TFLOP/s in every attempt; stage 2 above 20 ms at m = 15584 on the median of a clean run; a row where stage 2 is slower than split-K beyond noise.
